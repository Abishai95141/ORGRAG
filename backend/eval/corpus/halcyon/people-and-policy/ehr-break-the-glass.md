# Break-the-glass access to the EHR

**Owner:** Privacy Officer (Margaret Holloway) · **Applies to:** all workforce members with Meridian EHR access

Meridian EHR limits each user to the charts of patients they have a treatment, payment or operations relationship with — for example patients on their schedule, in their clinic, or assigned to their care team. "Break-the-glass" lets you open a chart outside those limits when a patient's care genuinely requires it.

## When it's appropriate

- A patient walks in unscheduled with an urgent problem and you need their history now.
- You are covering for a colleague whose patient calls with symptoms.
- A telehealth patient in another clinic's panel needs escalation and the assigned clinician is unavailable.

It is **never** appropriate for checking on a family member, a coworker, a public figure, or yourself — the minimum necessary rule in the HIPAA privacy policy still applies.

## How it works

1. Open the chart. Meridian shows the "Restricted record" warning.
2. Click **Emergency access** and pick a reason: *Emergency care*, *Coverage*, or *Other (explain)*.
3. Type a short justification. "Covering for Dr. Lee, patient called with chest pain" is good; "needed it" is not.
4. Access lasts until the end of your shift, up to **12 hours**. After that you must break the glass again.

No one has to approve break-the-glass in the moment — patient care comes first.

## What happens afterwards

- Every break-the-glass event is logged and reviewed by the Privacy Officer's team within **2 business days**.
- If the reason isn't clear, your manager is asked to confirm it.
- Inappropriate use is a privacy incident under the HIPAA privacy policy and may lead to sanctions up to termination.

## Tips

- If you find yourself breaking the glass for the same patient every week, ask your clinic manager to add them to your care team instead.
- Break-the-glass does not apply to sensitive records with extra legal protection (for example substance use treatment records). Those need the Privacy Officer.

# Sentinel and adverse event reporting policy

**Owner:** Quality & Patient Safety · **Approved by:** Patient Safety Committee · **Effective:** March 1, 2026

## Definitions

| Level | Name | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sentinel event** | Patient death or permanent harm not related to the natural course of illness; severe temporary harm; wrong-patient or wrong-site procedure; patient suicide within 72 hours of a visit |
| 2 | Adverse event with harm | Medication error requiring treatment; delayed follow-up of an abnormal result that changes care |
| 3 | No-harm event | Error reached the patient but caused no harm |
| 4 | Near miss | Error caught before it reached the patient |

When unsure, report at the higher level. QPS will reclassify if needed.

## Immediately

1. Make the patient safe. Follow the clinical escalation protocol (call 911 if needed).
2. Tell the **charge nurse** on shift. In clinics without a charge nurse on shift, tell the clinic manager. The charge nurse coordinates the immediate response: assigns who stays with the patient, who calls the family, and who secures equipment, medications or records involved.
3. For a sentinel event, the charge nurse notifies the on-call medical director within **1 hour**.

## Within 24 hours

- Anyone involved or who witnessed the event files a report in **SafeReport**. Reporting is never optional and never punished.
- The attending clinician discloses the event to the patient or family, with the charge nurse or medical director present.

## Root cause analysis

- Every Level 1 and Level 2 event gets a **root cause analysis (RCA)**. QPS charters the RCA team within 72 hours.
- The RCA must be **completed within 45 calendar days** of the event becoming known.
- RCAs use a just-culture approach: we look for system causes, not someone to blame.
- Completed RCAs, with their action plans, are reviewed by the **Patient Safety Committee**, chaired by the Chief Medical Officer, **Dr. Kenji Watanabe**. The committee meets monthly and tracks every action item to closure.

## External reporting

QPS decides whether an event must be reported to a state health department or accrediting body and makes those reports. Privacy breaches are separate and go to the Privacy Officer under the HIPAA privacy policy.

## Software-caused events

Events caused by software or patient-facing automation (for example IR-0412) follow this policy too. Fixes then go through clinical systems change control.

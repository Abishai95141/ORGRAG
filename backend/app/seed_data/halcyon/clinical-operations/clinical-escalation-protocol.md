# Clinical escalation protocol

This protocol defines when and how staff escalate a patient's condition. When in doubt, escalate — no one at Halcyon will ever be criticized for escalating too early.

## Red flags requiring immediate escalation

Call 911 and notify the on-site clinician immediately for:

- Chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw.
- Signs of stroke: facial droop, arm weakness, or speech difficulty (BE-FAST).
- SpO2 below 90% on room air.
- Systolic blood pressure above 180 or below 90 with symptoms.
- Active suicidal ideation with a plan or intent.

## Urgent same-day escalation

Notify the supervising physician within 30 minutes for:

- Fever above 39.4 °C (103 °F) in an adult, or any fever in an infant under 3 months.
- New confusion in a patient over 65.
- A critical lab value called in by the reference lab.
- Blood glucose above 400 mg/dL or below 60 mg/dL.

## Telehealth escalation

If a telehealth patient shows red-flag symptoms, the clinician keeps the video call open, confirms the patient's physical location, and calls 911 for that location on a separate line. The clinician stays on the call until emergency services arrive or the patient is handed off.

## Critical lab results

Critical results must reach the ordering clinician within 60 minutes. If the ordering clinician cannot be reached in 30 minutes, the result goes to the medical director on call. Every critical result notification is documented in the chart with the time and the person notified.

## Documentation

Every escalation is documented in the encounter note with the time, the symptoms observed, who was notified, and the outcome.
